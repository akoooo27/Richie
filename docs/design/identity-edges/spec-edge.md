## 1. Summary

The edge pages are the moments when sign-in can’t go on as planned: time ran out, the device was signed out, something failed on our side, a link is dead, the page doesn’t exist, the network is gone, a limit was reached, or the person is signing out. Each one is the Sign in frame with the form taken away (Part A §1.2): the same mascot, `h1` and footer positions, a badge on the mascot that says the outcome, a lede that says what we know with a real number, **one** filled action and always a way out. Nothing on these pages is red except a field or action failure line, nothing is decorative beyond the badge, and nothing reveals more about an account than Sign in already does. Fourteen pages (E1–E14) are covered by 21 boards; every other state is copy-only on a drawn layout (§5), and every state below has final copy. §3.6 also defines the time limit warning that every page under a timed state uses (B-29), drawn once on `Signin-timeout-warning`.

## 3. What every edge page shares

### 3.1 Shape

Outcome pages (E1, E4–E6, E8, E10, E13, E14) are `Signedout`’s shape: head (mascot with badge, `h1`, lede), one action stack, then at most one centred note and, where an action reports back, the `.ri-slot`. E11 adds the account chip under the lede. E7 and E9 are states of the form page `Main`. E2 is a settings dialog; E3 is Sign in with an alert. Measured: the mascot is at 689,64 and the `h1` top at 166 at 1440 on all 10 non-overlay WP3.2 boards, and 164,32 / 134 at 390, the same as `Main` and `Signedout`.

### 3.2 Badges (Part A §4.9)

| Page | Badge |
|---|---|
| E1 Timeout, E10 Limited | `clock-bold`, neutral |
| E4 Error, E5 Error-link | `exclamation-mark-bold`, neutral |
| E8 Offline | `wifi-slash-bold`, neutral |
| E13, E14 signed out | `check-bold`, ok |
| E6 Not found, E11 Sign out? | none (plain information, a question) |
| E2, E3, E7, E9 | none (states of a settings page or a form page) |

### 3.3 Copy

- Headlines are full sentences for outcomes and a question for the one choice page (E11). The exception is E6 "Page not found", kept because Part A §3.1 fixes the document title and it is the phrase people search for.
- Ledes are at most 2 lines at 390 (measured, §12.5); every lede says what we know and keeps the person’s place. Notes under the action add the real number or the one caveat.
- Failure lines use AS §4.0’s three patterns with Part A §4.2’s phrases. On sign-in surfaces every method button (passkey, Google, Microsoft) uses "sign you in" (B-6); "reach" and "use a passkey" are retired.
- Rate-limit lines are B-34’s: per address (L1) "You’ve asked for 5 codes in an hour.", per network (L2, L6) "This network has made 30 attempts in an hour.", each followed by the countdown or clock time where it fits (§4 E9, §7); other counts keep B-12’s pattern "This network has made {n} {things} in {window}."
- No toasts. No "oops", "invalid", "error code", "session", "logged out", "rate limit".

### 3.4 Focus and live regions (Part A §4.7)

- **Arrival:** outcome and question pages focus the `h1` (`tabindex="-1"`, no ring). E7 and E9 are reached by pressing a button, so focus stays on that button. E2 focuses its dialog title. E3 focuses the alert container (`role="group"`, `tabindex="-1"`, `aria-labelledby` its title, `aria-describedby` its description, no ring; B-38), so the description, which says what happens next, is read with the title.
- **One polite region per page:** the `.ri-slot` (`p[role=status]` + `p[role=alert]` in one cell) on E4, E7, E8, E9, E10, E11. E1, E5, E6, E13 and E14 have no in-page action that reports, so they have no slot.
- **Failures with focus on the pressed button** use the slot’s `role=alert` paragraph. **Countdowns are never live:** they are announced once at the start (the slot line or the `h1`) and once at 0:00.
- **Busy:** the pressed button keeps focus, shows the spinner and a gerund, and gets `aria-disabled="true"` (never `disabled`). **B-28:** the same gerund is written once into the slot’s polite paragraph as sr-only text (`<p class="ra-status" role="status"><span class="ra-sr">Signing out…</span></p>`) and cleared when the button returns to idle or the slot gets a result. No second live region.
- **Countdown buttons (B-27):** the ticking part is `aria-hidden` and the button carries a fixed sr suffix with the clock time, so the accessible name never changes while focused: `<button … aria-disabled="true" aria-describedby="slot-status"><span>Send code<span aria-hidden="true"> in 41:12</span></span><span class="ra-sr">, available at 4:53 PM</span></button>` → name "Send code, available at 4:53 PM" (it starts with the visible label, 2.5.3). The button is described by the slot line that gives the reason (A11Y-06). At 0:00 the label becomes the plain commit and the slot says the page’s 0:00 line once.
- **Countdown lengths (B-15):** rate-limit countdowns start at the server’s `Retry-After` / next-allowed time, m:ss up to 59:59; at an hour or more the label is a clock time ("Try again at 2:30 PM"). The 1:00 start is for the resend countdown only.

### 3.5 Document titles

"{h1} – Richie" with an en dash, never a name or an email (Part A §4.11), except when the `h1` already contains "Richie": then the `h1` alone (B-13: E3 "Sign in to Richie", E11 "Sign out of Richie?"). Listed per page below.

### 3.6 Time limit warning (B-29; the shared pattern)

WCAG 2.2.1 (Timing Adjustable). Written once here; WP3.4 (R3–R5) and any other page with a timed state point to this section instead of restating it. **Drawn once on `Signin-timeout-warning`** (a full copy of `Main`, 1440 × 900): email filled, the warning in the slot, **Keep going** focused (`.is-focused`, canvas only). Every timed page uses that board as its reference.

**Which timers, and where**

| Timed state | Length | Pages | When it runs out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pending sign-in | 20 minutes, sliding | Every identity page under a sign-in request: A1, A2, A3, A4, Verify, the passkey sign-in pages, R1, P4–P7 | E1 (`Timeout`), which keeps the email |
| Recovery draft (WP3.4) | 30 minutes, sliding | R3–R5, as spec-recovery says | WP3.4’s page |
| New-address confirmation (WP3.4) | 30 minutes, sliding | R4, R5, as spec-recovery says | WP3.4’s page |
| *Not covered:* the 5-minute email code | fixed | — | It is the authentication factor’s own lifetime (essential); "Send a new code" is the recovery |
| *Not covered:* a signed-in device | 14 days without use | settings | E2 |

**Sliding.** Any request that carries the timed state renews it server-side: page loads (GET), submits (POST), and a keep-alive `POST /signin/keepalive` (WP3.4: its own route) sent at most once a minute while the person types or changes a field. A countdown on the page (E9, E10) sends nothing. So a person who is working never sees the warning; it reaches people who walked away.

**Copy**

| Element | Copy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Warning, visible | Still there? This page times out in 2:00. **Keep going** | Appears 2:00 before the end; the m:ss ticks visually |
| Warning, accessible text | "Still there? This page times out at 4:32 PM. Keep going" | The m:ss is `aria-hidden`; a fixed sr clock time replaces it (B-27), so the region never re-announces |
| **Keep going** | plain button, inline at the end of the line | `.ri-edge-slot-action` (§9): drawn as text so the line stays 20 px and the slot never grows |
| After **Keep going** | You have 20 more minutes. | Status, 5 s, then the slot empties. WP3.4’s timers: "You have 30 more minutes." |
| Last warning (after the 10th **Keep going**) | This page times out in 2:00. After that, start again. | No button: the 10 extensions WCAG asks for are used |
| **Keep going** failed | We couldn’t add more time because you’re offline. / We couldn’t add more time. Check your connection. / We couldn’t add more time. Try again in a moment. | Alert, focus stays on **Keep going** |
| At 0:00 | — | The page goes to its "ran out" page (E1 for a sign-in), which says what was kept |

All strings measured against the slot rule (§7).

**Markup** (inside the page’s existing `.ri-slot`, never a new region):

```html
<p class="ra-status" role="status" id="slot-status"><span>Still there? This page times out
  <span aria-hidden="true">in 2:00</span><span class="ra-sr">at 4:32 PM</span>.
  <button data-slot="button" data-variant="plain" class="ri-edge-slot-action" type="button">Keep going</button></span></p>
```

**Behaviour**

- **Timer source.** Every response carries the state’s `expiresAt`; the page re-syncs its clock from it, and a `BroadcastChannel` shares it across tabs, so **Keep going** in one tab clears the warning in the others.
- **Live region.** The warning is written into the slot’s polite paragraph once, at 2:00. If the slot’s alert paragraph holds an older failure line, it is cleared (one message per cell). Countdown text is never live.
- **Focus** doesn’t move: the person may be away, or typing elsewhere (typing renews anyway). **Keep going** sits in the slot right after the page’s commit button, so it is the next Tab stop.
- **Time to respond:** 2 minutes (2.2.1 asks for at least 20 s), up to 10 extensions, each renewing the full length.
- **At 0:00** the page navigates to its "ran out" page (warned, so not a surprise), rather than leaving a page that looks alive but fails on the next press.
- **Layout.** Measured on `Signin-timeout-warning` in the rig build: at 1440, 390 and 320 the slot keeps its height (20, 20, 40: one line at 1440 and 390, two balanced lines at 320) and "or" stays where it is on `Main` (y 450 / 418 / 438), so the method buttons don’t move. A page under a timed state without a `.ri-slot` reserves one under its primary (Part A §4.6).

### E1 Sign-in timed out (`Timeout`, `-mobile`, `-dark`)

**Route** `/signin/expired?returnUrl=…` · **Title** "Your sign-in timed out – Richie" · **Badge** clock, neutral

| Element | Copy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `h1` | Your sign-in timed out | Same in every variant |
| Lede, code sign-in (**drawn**) | Start again with **jamie.rivera@northwind.coffee** to get a new code. | Email in `strong.ri-strong.ra-email-wrap` inside `translate="no"`; wraps after the @ (§4.8) |
| Lede, sign-up (copy) | Start again with **{email}** to create your account. | When the expired request came from Create account (A2, A3) |
| Lede, passkey (E1a, copy) | Start again and use your passkey. | No email: a discoverable passkey sign-in may never have had one |
| Lede, Google or Microsoft (E1b, copy) | Start again and choose Google. / Start again and choose Microsoft. | Provider callback after the request expired, or its correlation cookie expired (§10 D7) |
| Lede, no email known (copy) | Start again to sign in. | Fallback |
| Primary | Start again | Link; the one action |
| Note, code sign-in and sign-up after a code was sent (**drawn**) | A sign-in times out after 20 minutes without activity, so the code we sent earlier won’t work anymore. | Real number (P-4); "without activity" because the clock slides (B-29, COPY-15) |
| Note, passkey | A sign-in times out after 20 minutes without activity, and this one ended before your passkey finished. | |
| Note, provider | A sign-in times out after 20 minutes without activity, and this one ended before Google sent you back. | "Microsoft" variant likewise |
| Note, no code sent | A sign-in times out after 20 minutes without activity. | Sign-up left before any code was sent |

**Behaviour**

- **Trigger.** The pending sign-in ran out: 20 minutes passed with no activity (COV-36, B-29: any GET, POST or the typing keep-alive renews it, §3.6). Two ways to arrive: the person ignored the 2-minute warning and the page navigated here at 0:00, or the device slept past it and the next request (a submit on Verify, the passkey assertion, an A1–A3 submit, P5–P6, a page load) found it expired, or found a `returnUrl` that was valid at load giving a `null` authorization context (Part A §3.2); the server answers 303 here. The provider callback fails the same way when its correlation cookie has expired (B-1: E1b, not E5).
- **What it keeps.** The pending sign-in (a data-protected cookie, never the query string) holds the email, the method and the client id. The email is shown; the method picks the lede.
- **Start again** goes to the client’s login start with `login_hint={email}`: `Client.InitiateLoginUri` when set, else `ClientUri`, else the Richie app root (which issues a fresh authorize request). The person lands on Sign in with the email filled (A1) and presses **Send code** once; a sign-up restart lands on Create account with the email and names filled (`prompt=create`). The old `returnUrl` can’t be resumed (Part A §3.3 rule 4).
- **Focus** the `h1` on arrival. No live region. Help is the footer link.

**Reveals** nothing: the email shown is the one this browser typed in this sign-in. **Limit** none.

**Rationale.** People reach this page by doing the right thing too late, often after typing a correct code, so it reads as a restart, not an error: a clock badge, not an exclamation mark, and no red. The lede carries the two facts that make restarting painless (we kept your email, a new code is one press away); the 20-minute number and the reason the old code fails sit in the note, so the lede stays at two lines at 390 even with the email in it. One button, because there is exactly one useful thing to do.

### E2 Signed out while in settings (`Session-expired`, `-mobile`)

**Route** a dialog over `/account/*` (drawn over Profile) · **Title** unchanged (the settings page’s)

| Element | Copy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dialog title | You were signed out | `h2`, `tabindex="-1"`, initial focus, no ring |
| Page behind (**drawn**) | Profile with unsaved edits: First name "Jay", the dirty action row (**Cancel**, **Save**), the preview following the typed name | The state the description talks about, as AS `Profile-save-failed` draws it without its error line (the save never reached the server) |
| Description, unsaved changes (**drawn**) | Your changes on this page aren’t saved yet. Sign in again within 30 minutes and we’ll put them back. | Real number from Part A §3.3 rule 6 |
| Description, nothing unsaved (copy) | Sign in again to keep using your account settings. | A load, a navigation or the visibility check returned 401 |
| Primary | Sign in again | Link |
| Secondary (outline) | Go to Richie | Link to the Richie app root. "Go", not "Back": E2 is an edge page (B-14) |
| Description, a setting that saves on change (copy) | Your last change wasn’t saved. Sign in again, then change it once more. | Preferences: the control that failed has already reverted (AS §4.5), so there is nothing to put back |
| Settings status after the restore (copy) | We put back what you typed. Save to keep it. | Polite, in the restored form’s status line (Profile: the action row’s `p[role=status]`, with Cancel and Save shown as in the dirty state). Measured 1 line at AS’s 458 px rule (§7) |

**Behaviour**

- **Trigger.** Any settings request through the BFF returns 401: the device’s sign-in ended (14 days without use or 90 days at most, Part A §8 Q2), another device signed this one out (E14, Security’s device list), or a recovery or deletion ended every session. The app also calls `/bff/user` when the tab becomes visible again (`visibilitychange`), so a person returning to an idle tab sees the dialog before they type anything (the "nothing unsaved" variant). We don’t say which cause it was (§11 QE-2).
- **Unsaved values.** On a 401 during a save, the page writes its dirty page-level fields (not open dialogs, never codes or typed confirmation phrases) to `sessionStorage` under `richie:draft:{path}` with a 30-minute expiry, then opens the dialog. **Which pages keep a draft (COV-29):**

  | Settings page | Kept | Where "We put back…" shows | Focus after the restore |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Profile | First name, Last name (the name form) | The action row’s polite status, with **Cancel** and **Save** shown (dirty state) | The `h1` (AS page arrival); the next Tab stop is the photo row, then First name |
  | Preferences | Nothing: each control saves on change; the failed one reverts | — (E2 uses the "setting that saves on change" description) | — |
  | Sign-in and security, Connected apps, Data and privacy | Nothing: their inputs live in dialogs, which are never restored | — | — |

- **The Richie app** (not settings) owns its own session end: it is outside identity (how-we-design §6, "Belongs to the Richie app"). It may reuse this dialog and send `signin_reason=session_ended`; Sign in then shows E3’s "Sign in to go back to Richie." The settings link callback (`/account/security/link/callback`) that finds an ended session loads Sign-in and security, which gets a 401 and shows E2 (nothing unsaved), with the same hint.
- **Sign in again** → `/bff/login?returnUrl={this page}` with `login_hint` (the BFF sets `LoginHint` on the OIDC challenge) and `signin_reason=session_ended`, which Sign in shows as E3. After sign-in the page restores the draft into its fields **unsaved**, shows "We put back what you typed. Save to keep it." and deletes the draft. Open dialogs are not reopened.
- **Go to Richie** → the Richie app root in the same tab. The draft stays for its 30 minutes: coming back to the same settings page in this tab and signing in within that time restores it; after that it is dropped silently (the description already said 30 minutes).
- **Escape, outside click and Back.** Escape and outside clicks do nothing; Browser Back navigates normally (to a page that will also show this dialog, or out of settings). Tab cycles between the two buttons. See §10 D3 for why this departs from AS dialog rules.
- **Layout.** suiss `modal`: a 448 px dialog at ≥768 (footer: **Go to Richie** outline, then **Sign in again**), a floating drawer below 768 (stacked, **Sign in again** on top). No close button. No form, because the dialog has no input. **The drawer has no grab handle:** it can’t be swiped away (`dismissible={false}`, D3), and a handle would say it can; the board marks it `data-dismissible="false"` and edges.css drops the handle and its room (24 above the title). DOM order: commit first below 768 (B-30); the board keeps the shared desktop DOM (B-41).

**Reveals** nothing. **Limit** none.

**Rationale.** The worst outcome of an ended session is silent loss: a person types a new name, presses Save and gets an error, or worse, a sign-in page that throws their typing away. The dialog says what happened in four words, then answers the only question that matters at that moment (is my typing lost?) with a promise and a real limit. It stays a dialog over the page, not a redirect, so the person can see the work they’re protecting; and it can’t be dismissed into a page that can no longer save.
